Abstract—Solar photovoltaic (PV) technology has become a 
key component in the global transition toward sustainable energy, 
with countries such as India witnessing rapid growth in solar 
power deployment due to abundant solar resources and increasing 
energy demand. E fficient utilization of available solar irradiance 
is therefore critical to maximize energy generation from installed 
PV systems. In this context, bifacial photovoltaic modules have 
gained significant attention as they are capable of capturing solar 
irradi ance from both the front and rear surfaces, leading to higher 
energy yield compared to conventional monofacial panels. 
However, the performance advantage of bifacial PV systems 
strongly depends on installation parameters such as module tilt 
angle, ground s urface albedo, and mounting height, which 
influence the amount of reflected and incident irradiance received 
by the rear side of the module. This paper presents a 
computational framework for determining the optimal 
configuration of bifacial PV installation s for a given geographical 
location. A user -oriented front -end platform is developed where 
inputs such as city and date are provided, and corresponding 
hourly irradiance data are retrieved from the NASA database. 
Parametric analyses are then performed by v arying key 
installation parameters including tilt angle, surface albedo, and 
mounting height. The framework evaluates the resulting energy 
output and identifies the configuration that maximizes power 
generation for the selected location. The proposed appro ach 
enables location -specific optimization of bifacial PV systems and 
provides a practical tool for improving system design and 
deployment strategies.  
Keywords —Solar Photovoltaic, Bifacial PV, parametric 
analysis, frontend platform, strategic installation.  
I. INTRODUCTION  
      Solar photovoltaic (PV) technology has emerged as one of 
the most promising renewable energy solutions for addressing 
the increasing global demand for clean and sustainable 
electricity generation. Continuous advancements in PV modul e 
technology and system design have enabled solar power 
systems to be deployed across a wide range of climatic regions. 
Conventional monofacial photovoltaic modules generate 
electricity only from solar radiation incident on the front 
surface of the panel. A s a result, a por tion of the available solar energy in the surrounding environment, such as reflected and 
diffuse radiation, remains unutilized. Bifacial photovoltaic 
modules have been  introduced as an effective alternative 
capable of converting    irradiance incident on both  the front and 
rear surfaces of the  module, thereby increasing the total energy 
harvested from the solar resource  [1], [2]. Several studies have 
demonstrated that bifacial PV systems can achieve higher 
energy yields compared to conventional modules when 
appropriate installation configurations are adopted [3]. 
Comprehensive reviews of bifacial PV technology highlight 
that rear -side irradiance contribution is strongly influenced by 
environmental and geometric parameters including ground 
reflectivity, module tilt angle, and installation height [1], [4]. 
      The performance of bifacial photovoltaic systems depends 
on the complex interaction between solar radiation, ground 
reflection, and system geometry. Numerous studies have 
investigated the influence of these parameters on the overall 
energy generation of bi facial PV modules. For instance, tilt 
angle has been identified as a critical parameter affecting the 
balance between front -side and rear -side irradiance capture, 
with optimal angles varying depending on geographical 
location and solar path character istics  [5], [6]. Similarly, 
ground surface reflectivity, commonly referred to as albedo, 
plays a crucial role in determining the amount of radiation 
reflected toward the rear surface of the module [7], [8]. 
Experimental investigations and modeling studies have shown 
that higher albedo surfaces can significantly increase rear -side 
irradiance and consequently improve overall energy generation 
[9], [10]. In addition to tilt and albedo, the elevation of modules 
above the ground also affects the view factor between the 
module and reflective surfaces, which can influence the amount 
of rear -side radiation received by the panel [11]. Field 
experiments conducted under various climatic conditions 
further confirm that environmental factors and installation 
configurations collectively determine the bifacial gain 
achievable in real PV systems [12], [13]. 
      Despite the growing body of research on bifacial 
photovoltaic systems, several challenges remain in determining 
the optimal configuration of these systems under varying 

environmental conditions. Many existing studies have focused 
on individual parameters such as tilt angle, albedo, or module 
elevation, often evaluating their effects independently rather 
than considering their combined influence on system 
performance [5], [8], [9]. Other investigations have developed 
modeling approaches or global performance assessments of 
bifacial PV systems; however, these studies are often limited to 
predefined system configurations or specific climatic 
conditions [4]. Furthermore, research on the design of large -
scale bifacial solar farms indicates that multiple parameters, 
including module orientation, mounting height, and system 
geometry, must be optimized simultaneously to achieve 
maximum energy output  [14]. Consequently, there is a need for 
a systematic analytical framework that can evaluate the 
combined influence of key installation parameters using 
location -specific solar irradiance data. Such an approach would 
enable the identification of optimal configu rations that 
maximize energy generation for a given site. The present work 
addresses this requirement by developing a parametric analysis 
framework that investigates the influence of tilt angle, ground 
albedo, and mounting height on bifacial PV system 
performance in order to determine the optimal configuration for 
maximizing power output.  This paper is divided into 4 sections 
– first is the introduction, second is the methodology, third are 
the results and discussion and fourth is the conclusion.  
 
II. METHODOLOGY  
This paper proposes a parametric analysis for the optimal 
configuration of bifacial photovoltaic systems. Tilt angle, 
mounting height of the panel, surface albedo and view factor are 
the parameters that are addressed  as shown in Fig 1 . A Simulink

was incorporated in the user frontend to calculate the max power

B. Simulink Model  
The bifacial photovoltaic (PV) system is modeled in 
MATLAB/Simulink  as shown in Fig. 3 to analyze the electrical 
performance of the module under varying environmental and 
installation conditions. The model incorporates key parameters 
such as global horizontal irradiance (GHI), ground reflectivity 
(albedo), tilt angle, mounting height, and t emperature.   
The input parameters are processed through a MATLAB 
function block ( calculate_irradiance ), which determines the 
effective irradiance 𝐺𝑒𝑓𝑓 received by the module , the one as in 
(1). The resulting effective irradiance value is then supplied to 
the PV Array block as the irradiance input 𝐼𝑟. The PV Array 
block represents the electrical model of the photovoltaic module and produces the output current and voltage 
corresponding to the applied operating conditions.  To obtain 
the electrical characteristics of the PV system, a controlled 
voltage source is used to perform an I –V sweep across the PV 
module terminals. The voltage sweep is generated using a ramp 
signal ( 𝑉𝑟𝑎𝑚𝑝 ), which gradually varies the applied voltage 
across the PV array over the simulation interval. As the voltage 
changes, the PV module produces corresponding current 
values, allowing the complete I –V characteristic curve of the 
module to be obtained. The me asured current ( 𝐼𝑚𝑒𝑎𝑠 ) and 
voltage ( 𝑉𝑚𝑒𝑎𝑠 ) signals are collected through a bus creator block 
and forwarded to subsequent processing stages.  he first scope 
plots the current –voltage (I–V) characteristics of the bifacial 
PV module, while the second scope plots the power –voltage 
(P–V) characteristics.

C. Proposed Frontend Platform Framework  
The proposed framework is designed to evaluate and 
determine the optimal configuration of bifacial photovoltaic 
(PV) systems by integrating location -based solar irradiance 
data with parametric performance analysis  as shown in Fig. 4. 
The framework consists of four primary layers: the user 
interface, backend application, analysis and simulation engine, 
and the results dashboard.   
The process begins at the user interface, where the user 
provides the necessary input parameters required for the 
analysis. These inputs include the geographical location in the 
form of a selected city, the desired date range for analysis, and 
key system c onfiguration parameters such as module tilt angle, 
mounting height, and ground surface type.  Once the input 
parameters are specified, the backend application processes 
these inputs and generates a request for solar irradiance data. 
The irradiance data is obtained through the NASA POWER 
API, which provides location -specific solar radiation data for  
the selected city and time period.   
The irradiance dataset is then passed to the analysis and 
simulation engine, which performs the computational 
evaluation of the bifacial PV system. This module integrates the 
irradiance data with the user -defined installation parameters to 
simulate the ele ctrical behavior of the bifacial PV system. The 
simulation process evaluates the current –voltage (I–V) and 
power–voltage (P –V) characteristics of the system and 
calculates key performance indicators such as output power and 
bifacial gain under different co nfiguration settings.  


Finally, the computed results are presented through a results 
dashboard, which provides a structured visualization of the 
system performance. The dashboard displays the maximum 
power output and bifacial gain values in tabular form, along 
with graphical rep resentations of the I –V and P –V 
characteristics.

parametric sweep was performed over a range of geometric 
configurations. The module mounting height was varied from 
50 cm to 450 cm with increments of 50 cm, whi le the tilt angle 
was varied from 10 ℃ to 50 ℃ in steps of 10 ℃. The azimuth 
angle of the PV module was fixed at 180 ° for south facing

IV. CONCLUSION  
      This paper analyses the optimal configuration of bifacial 
photovoltaic (PV) systems through a computational framework 
that integrates location -specific irradiance data with parametric 
performance evaluation. The developed platform utilizes 
irradiance data retrieved from the NASA POWER database and 
processes it through a MATLAB -based simulation engine 
implementing the irradiance and electrical models of bifacial 
PV modules. The results indicate that the rear -side irradiance 
contribution is significantly infl uenced by the combined effects 
of surface reflectivity, module tilt angle, and mounting height. 
Higher ground albedo leads to increased rear irradiance due to 
greater reflection of incident solar radiation toward the rear 
surface of the module. In contrast , variations in mounting 
height with other parameters fixed result in relatively smaller 
changes in rear gain, as the geometric view factor between the 
ground and the module does not increase substantially beyond 
certain elevations. Similarly, changes in t ilt angle modify the
